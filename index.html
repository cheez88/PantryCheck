    How the score works

    Every food gets up to 100 points from three equal parts:

    Processing (34 points) uses the NOVA system. Fresh and whole foods score highest, ultra-processed foods lowest.

    Nutrition (33 points) uses the 2024 Nutri-Score, which penalises sugar, saturated fat and salt, and rewards fibre, protein and fruit and vegetables.

    Additives (33 points) loses 2 points for most additives and 8 points for additives with health concerns, like certain artificial colours, preservatives and sweeteners. Natural ones like citric acid and pectin cost nothing.

    Ultra-processed foods are capped at 60, so they can never show green. 70 and above is an everyday food, 40 to 69 is a sometimes food, and below 40 is a treat.

    If a barcode isn't in the database, you can photograph the label instead. Claude reads the nutrition panel and ingredients, you check the numbers, and the result is saved on your phone for next time.

    Product data comes from Open Food Facts, a free community database. Scores are a guide for making better choices, not medical advice.
